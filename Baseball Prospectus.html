The Orioles Fatten Their Midsection with Freddy Peralta

Justin Klugh
November 24, 2026
Share:

Still in need of an ace and a dominant no. 2, the Orioles watched their former starter Trevor Rogers get signed by the Cubs before locking down free agent Freddy Peralta on a five-year, $125 million deal with no opt-outs or deferred money. After the Mets had gotten their man in Tarik Skubal, the free agent starting pitching market started to figure itself out, and Peralta was near the top of the list.

The Mets would have loved to face a pitcher like Freddy Peralta in 2026. Enthusiasm for the trade they made for him in January, in which they sent two top prospects to Milwaukee, eroded quickly. They watched Fastball Freddy give up seven hits and three walks in a losing effort to a terrible Rockies team in April, blow his only seven-inning start for New York with a 4-1 loss to the Marlins in May, and earn ten runs in less than three innings in an ass-kicking by the Phillies in June. The Mets could have signed Peralta to be their no. 4 starter and he probably still would have been underwhelming.

When the Rays come calling, it can feel like they know something you don’t. Whoever they’re after on your roster, the more underperforming the player is, the more ominous Tampa’s desire seems. But by the trade deadline, the Mets had watched all they could stand from Peralta and accepted his poor output and expiring contact as part of another lost, very expensive season.

So it was off to Tampa at the deadline for Peralta, and in time, the Mets got their wish. They’d face a pitcher like Freddy Peralta on September 1.
