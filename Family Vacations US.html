5 Florida Garden Tours To Enjoy Before Winter Takes Hold

Thomas Edison spent some of his final years trying to turn goldenrod, a common roadside weed, into rubber.

The lab where his team chased that wild idea still stands in a Florida garden you can stroll through today.

That kind of surprise pops up again and again on this list.

One garden has a marble tower holding 60 bronze bells, and another keeps a whole conservatory of butterflies.

Fall feels like the sweet spot for exploring them. The sticky summer heat finally backs off, and shady paths start feeling friendly instead of steamy.

I love that one easy afternoon can mix history, rare plants, and a few great stories.

So which garden is getting a spot on your calendar before the cooler months settle across the state?

Bok Tower Gardens

A marble tower that plays music sounds like something out of a storybook. In Lake Wales, it’s real, and it rises 205 feet above the trees.

The gardens were dedicated in 1929 by President Calvin Coolidge. Edward Bok, a well-known magazine editor, wanted to create a peaceful spot where people could enjoy beauty.

Inside the tower hangs a carillon with 60 bronze bells. Daily concerts float across the grounds, so you can wander and listen at the same time.

Frederick Law Olmsted Jr. designed the landscape. His father helped shape Central Park, so great garden design clearly ran in the family.

Shady paths curl past ferns, palms, and a reflection pool that mirrors the tower. Pinewood Estate, a 1930s Mediterranean-style home, also sits on the property for curious visitors to explore.

The gardens sit on Iron Mountain, one of the highest points in peninsular Florida. Cooler fall weather makes that gentle climb much easier on your legs.

Bring a camera and some patience for the bell music. That tower looks stunning from nearly any angle you choose.

Address: 1151 Tower Blvd, Lake Wales, FL 33853

Fairchild Tropical Botanic Garden

Butterflies landing on your shoulder count as a pretty great welcome. At this Coral Gables garden, the Wings of the Tropics conservatory makes that a real possibility.

The garden opened in 1938 and covers 83 acres. It was named for David Fairchild, a plant explorer who traveled the world collecting useful and unusual plants.

Palms are a huge part of the story here. You’ll find rows of them in shapes and sizes you probably never knew existed.

Want to save some steps? Fairchild offers narrated tram tours that introduce visitors to the garden’s history and plant collections.

Check the current schedule and reservation requirements before your visit, since availability can vary.

Lakes, lawns, and rainforest-style plantings give the place lots of variety. One minute you’re in open sunshine, and the next you’re under a thick green canopy.

The garden also does serious science work on rare and threatened plants. So your visit supports more than a nice afternoon out.

Fall is a smart time to go in South Florida. The air feels lighter, and those long trails suddenly seem much shorter.

Address: 10901 Old Cutler Rd, Coral Gables, FL 33156

Marie Selby Botanical Gardens

Some plants here don’t even bother with soil. Selby Gardens is famous for epiphytes, which grow on trees and pull moisture from the air.

That means orchids and bromeliads take center stage. The Tropical Conservatory shows them off in bursts of pink, purple, and bright green.

Marie and William Selby once owned this land along Sarasota Bay. After Marie passed, her property became a public garden, which opened in 1975.

Outside the conservatory, giant banyan trees stretch their roots in every direction they can find. Kids tend to treat them like nature’s own jungle gym, so keep an eye on little climbers.

The bayfront views add a breezy bonus to the visit. You can wander from shaded paths to open water in just a few steps.

Want a second adventure? Selby also runs the Historic Spanish Point campus in nearby Osprey, which mixes Florida history with coastal gardens.

Autumn in Sarasota brings friendlier temperatures for strolling. It’s a lovely excuse to slow down and actually read those plant labels.

Address: 1534 Mound Street, Sarasota, FL 34236

Edison and Ford Winter Estates

Thomas Edison invented the phonograph and helped light up homes. Down in Fort Myers, he also tried to grow rubber from goldenrod.

Edison first came to Fort Myers in 1885 and built a winter home called Seminole Lodge. His friend Henry Ford later bought the house next door, which made for some pretty famous neighbors.

The gardens cover about 21 acres along the Caloosahatchee River. You’ll stroll past tropical plants, flowering trees, and the homes where these big thinkers relaxed.

Don’t miss the giant banyan tree near the entrance. Harvey Firestone gave it to Edison in 1925, and it has grown into a true monster of a tree.

The Edison Botanic Research Laboratory is another highlight. Edison, Ford, and Firestone teamed up to search for a homegrown source of rubber in the United States.

Guided tours share stories about the families and their experiments. It’s a garden visit and a science lesson rolled into one.

Fall is a great time to explore Southwest Florida. Bring curiosity, because the inventions here are just as fun as the flowers.

Address: 2350 McGregor Blvd, Fort Myers, FL 33901

Vizcaya Museum and Gardens

Ever wanted to feel like European royalty without leaving Miami? Vizcaya makes that daydream surprisingly easy.

Businessman James Deering built this waterfront estate on Biscayne Bay between 1914 and 1922. He wanted a winter home inspired by the grand villas of Italy and France.

The formal gardens cover about 10 acres. Neat hedges, stone statues, fountains, and hidden corners make them feel like an outdoor maze of art.

Out in the bay sits a stone barge shaped like a boat. It works as a breakwater and makes a striking backdrop for photos.

The main house holds rooms filled with antiques and fancy decor. You can tour inside, then wander back out to the gardens for fresh air.

Vizcaya is a National Historic Landmark, so it carries real weight in American history. It also happens to be one of the prettiest places in South Florida to spend a slow afternoon.

Cooler weather makes the stroll more relaxing. Just leave room on your phone, because you’ll want plenty of pictures.

Address: 3251 S Miami Ave, Miami, FL 33129
