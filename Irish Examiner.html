Munster Abroad: I still can't bring myself to leave New York City after 26 years

In the first of a new series, Sarah Horgan meets Munster people who have made new lives overseas
Wed, 25 Nov, 2026 - 10:26
Sarah Horgan
  
  

It was only ever meant to be a holiday, but 26 years after visiting a friend this side of the water, I still can’t bring myself to leave New York.

I always knew I wanted to be an actor. After graduating from Coláiste Choilm secondary school in Ballincollig, I enrolled in a full-time acting course with the college then known as Coláiste Stiofáin Naofa. I later began theatre studies with the Inchicore College of Further Education.

It was flying out to visit a friend in the Big Apple that changed everything for me. He was working in a pub at the time and I thought it might be a bit of craic to go visit him in 2000. I fell in love with the place and never came back.

I got a bit of work in a pub here on the Upper East Side called Ryan’s Daughter. I just settled down and got into work here before auditioning for acting roles.

It was similar to my life back in Dublin that saw me do theatre while working in a bar by night.

I took over the pub business in 2010. My love for theatre played a huge part in what I was doing.

We would actually stage plays on the second floor of the bar, featuring the likes of renowned actor, comedian, and broadcaster Niall Tóibín — who passed away in 2019.

It was while working and running the bar that I got involved with Red Dead Redemption 2. I both voiced and performed motion capture for the character of Seán MacGuire in the video game, which was released in 2018.

The game is the third entry in the Red Dead series and a prequel to the 2010 game Red Dead Redemption. Set in a fictionalised depiction of the US in 1899, it follows the exploits of Arthur Morgan, an outlaw and member of the Van der Linde gang, who must face the challenges of a declining Wild West, while dealing with government forces, rival gangs, and other adversaries along the way.

My character Sean MacGuire is an Irish outlaw, and he’s a very funny character to play. He has a Dublin accent, inspired by my uncles.

In the years I was working on the video game, I was getting a couple of small bits on television and very consistent theatre work.

Art was imitating life for me, as I even played a bartender on Law & Order: Special Victims Unit. In March of 2018, I got to do a play with Matthew Broderick of Ferris Bueller’s Day Off fame.

We starred in a play together called The Seafarer at the Irish Repertory Theatre, which is an off-Broadway venue for Irish and Irish-American drama in New York City. They have been very good to me over the years. I had given up the bar business that same year. However, covid shut down all the theatres and bars in 2020.

It was during the pandemic that I took over a theatre company called ORIGIN, which is based here in New York. We presented Irish and Irish-inspired plays to New York online audiences. When the pandemic came to an end, we brought our productions to theatres all around the city. We staged some wonderful plays over the space of five years. There were close to 100 plays as well as readings and performances.

Covid was undoubtedly a tough period, but it also happened to be the year I married the love of my life, Jessica in 2021, who is originally from Connecticut. We had met when she was a customer at the bar where I worked in 2013, but it was a few years before anything happened.

When it comes to my career, she is very supportive. I’m currently starring in Parcel From America, a Christmas musical. Set in a remote parish in 1950s rural West Limerick, it tells the story of a community whose lives are changed by a parcel sent by relatives from America.

Since moving here, probably the biggest change for me has been my eating habits. I refuse to eat pizza in Ireland or anywhere basically outside New York. I love the pizza in New York, which is why you will never catch me eating it when I’m back home.

Of course, I still love coming home to Cork. What I miss most is probably the craic, but the closeness of the people in Cork is something I often yearn for. I love how people walking down MacCurtain Street say hello whether they know each other or not.

I miss the sense of humour and the fact that everyone just gets each other. Even though my accent has never really changed too much, I’ll still get a slagging when I go home for saying silly things like sidewalk instead of footpath or trunk instead of boot. No matter what happens or how old I get, I will always be just your typical feen from Ballincollig, original Knocknaheeny. I think staying true to who you are is what Cork people are all about.
